Defensible Space

Defensible space is the buffer you create between your home and the wildland fuels around it. Done well, it slows fire spread, gives crews a safe place to defend a structure, and protects the native habitat you actually want to keep. Done poorly — clear-cutting, scalping, indiscriminate spraying — it can leave the land more vulnerable than before.

We work the way CAL FIRE recommends: zone by zone, plant by plant. Zone 0 (0–5 ft from the structure) is kept ember-resistant. Zone 1 (5–30 ft) is thinned and spaced. Zone 2 (30–100 ft) is managed for fuel continuity. We document what we remove, what we keep, and why — so you have a record before fire season and a plan for the years that follow.

How We Plan Your Defensible-Space Zones

Every property gets a free site visit before any work is scoped. We walk the parcel with you, identify the structures, access roads, water sources, and ignition pathways, and flag native vegetation worth keeping. You get a written quote with the work broken out by zone — so you can stage it across one season or several.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • How does land stewardship help your property?
    Land stewardship — done over years, not weekends — protects what makes your property worth living on. Defensible space lowers wildfire risk; biochar improves soil and locks carbon; native plants restore habitat and reduce maintenance over time. We design the work so it compounds: each visit makes the next one easier.
  • What is biochar and why does it matter?
    Biochar is charcoal made from forest biomass under controlled, low-oxygen burning. Mixed into soil it holds water, supports microbial life, and stores carbon for centuries. We produce it on-site from the woody material we remove during defensible-space and fuels-reduction work — so the byproduct of one job becomes the input for the next.
  • Do you offer bilingual EN/ES trainings?
    Yes — every workshop and crew briefing we run is offered in English and Spanish. Trainings cover defensible-space layout, biochar production, native-plant identification, and crew safety. We work with landowners, fire safe councils, and partner organizations across Northern California.
